# 9 Surprising Facts About Bras Every Woman Needs to Know

Let's face it: almost every woman is familiar with underwear essentials, but how much do you actually know about the history and bra facts surrounding this daily necessity? Bras are an everyday piece of clothing we become familiar with in our preteen years. We begin in "training bras" and end up wearing supportive, lacy, and comfortable styles in a wide variety of shapes and sizes. If you want to learn surprising facts about bras, fascinating history, and bra information you never knew, read on!

## 1. Hanky Panky

Among the most interesting facts about bras is the story behind one of the first modern bra designs. In 1914, New York socialite Mary Phelps Jacob created a lightweight backless design using two silk handkerchiefs and a pink ribbon. She later patented her invention and sold the design to the [Warner Brothers Corset Company](https://www.warners.com/). While it was not the very first breast support garment in history, it became one of the most commercially successful early brassiere styles. Creating a custom piece like this is a fun project, much like creating [unique fashion accessories for your wardrobe](https://fashion.allwomenstalk.com/unique-tights-that-you-must-have-in-your-wardrobe/).

## 2. The Term "brassiere"...

Did you ever wonder how do you spell brassiere or where the word originates? The term "brassiere" was first printed in [Vogue magazine](https://www.vogue.com/) in 1907. Derived from an Old French word meaning "arm protector" or "support," it describes the essential function of supporting the bust. Over the following decades, common usage naturally shortened the term to the simple word "bra" that we use today.

## 3. Cretan Bras

Long before modern lingerie was manufactured, the ancient civilization of Crete introduced early forms of support garments. Women in ancient Minoan Crete wore corsets and band-like breast supports hundreds of years before similar garments appeared in Europe. Interestingly, Minoan depictions show garments that supported while leaving the bust exposed, as seen in ancient statues of the Minoan Snake Goddess.

## 4. Cups Invented

It was not until the 1920s that bra design truly evolved when Ida and William Rosenthal, founders of Maidenform, introduced standardized cup sizes. Before this innovation, breast support garments were largely one-size-fits-all options that provided minimal shaping. Standardizing cup letters completely transformed women's comfort and fit. For proper support, it helps to understand key bra components:

- **Band:** Provides roughly 80% of the bra's structural support around the ribcage.

- **Cups:** Enclose and shape the bust area for comfort and symmetry.

- **Straps:** Keep the cups flush against the body without digging into shoulders.

## 5. BB's

In the early 1900s, early brassieres were often referred to as "bust bodices" or "BBs." It is amusing to picture women of that era telling their friends, "Let's go shopping for some new BBs!" or selecting a new bust bodice for an evening out. This historical terminology shows how quickly lingerie language has evolved over the last century.

## 6. Frederick

Anyone who appreciates a supportive padded or push-up bra can thank Frederick Mellinger, founder of [Frederick's of Hollywood](https://www.fredericks.com/). He invented the first padded bra in 1947, followed by the first push-up bra in 1948. Frederick is also credited with pioneering front-hook bras and other popular modern lingerie designs that offer both style and function.

## 7. Brassier Revolution

In 1968, the bra became a central symbol during feminist demonstrations at the Miss America pageant in Atlantic City. Protesters protested against restrictive beauty standards by symbolically tossing bras, girdles, and hair spray into a "Freedom Trash Can." Although media myths claimed bras were set on fire, police rules prohibited open flames, so the garments were simply thrown away. This moment marked a dramatic shift in how society viewed brassieres and women's clothing choices.

## 8. Jock Strap Bra

In 1977, the very first sports bra was created by Lisa Lindahl, Polly Palmer Smith, and Hinda Miller. The prototype was cleverly stitched together from two jockstraps! Today, specialized sports bras are an essential piece of athletic wear for running, jumping, and workouts, providing crucial support and preventing tissue strain during high-impact activities. ## 9. Big TaTa's

Did you know bra sizing now extends up to cup size N and beyond? Studies show the average cup size in North America has increased from a 34B to a 36DD over recent decades due to changes in body measurements, fitting awareness, and manufacturing. Wearing the correct bra size is critical for maintaining posture and preventing upper back strain.
